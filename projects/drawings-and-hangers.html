Princeton Carbon Works · Summer 2026

GD&T Frame Drawings and Derailleur Hanger Replicas

Manufacturing drawings specifying the critical dimensions of a carbon bicycle frame, and SolidWorks models of the Shimano direct-mount and SRAM UDH derailleur hangers, 3D printed and fitted to an existing bike.

Objective

Specify the frame interfaces that add-on components depend on, and model the two industry-standard derailleur hanger interfaces.

Contribution

GD&T manufacturing drawings for the frame; measurement and SolidWorks modeling of the Shimano DH and SRAM UDH; 3D-printed replicas of both.

Result

Dimensioned drawings delivered for manufacturing. Both hanger replicas fit an existing frame and derailleur and were installed on the bike.

Manufacturing drawings for a carbon frame

2D SolidWorks drawings were prepared to specify the frame dimensions that brake, drivetrain, and cockpit components depend on, so that each add-on part interfaces correctly with the frame.

  • Dimensions were referenced to datums at the bottom bracket and rear dropouts.
  • Critical interfaces, such as brake mounting holes, were held to tight tolerances, while less critical depths and sizes were given wider ones. GD&T callouts communicate this intent to the manufacturer.
  • Fork and rear dropouts can deflect while the carbon cures, so these features were given wider tolerances than a machined part would need.

Reverse engineering of the Shimano DH and SRAM UDH

The Shimano direct-mount hanger and the SRAM Universal Derailleur Hanger (UDH) were modeled in SolidWorks from measurements taken with calipers, a protractor, and hand sketches. Both models were then 3D printed to check them against real hardware.

Figure 1. SolidWorks models of the Shimano DH and SRAM UDH replicas.

Outcomes

  • Dimensioned manufacturing drawings with GD&T callouts were delivered for the carbon frame.
  • Both 3D-printed hanger replicas interfaced correctly with an existing frame and derailleur and were installed on the bike.
