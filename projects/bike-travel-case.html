An ultralight bike travel case

Hard bike cases protect well, but they are heavy enough to eat most of an airline's weight allowance before the bike goes in. I'm designing a case with a contoured carbon/foam sandwich shell that follows the bike's shape, with a target of about half the weight of the lightest case on the market.

The idea

A sandwich panel, with thin carbon skins on a lightweight foam core, gets most of its bending stiffness from separating the skins, not from adding material. Shaping the shell to the bike's outline also removes the empty volume a box-shaped case carries around. Putting the two together should give a case that is stiff where it needs to be and light everywhere else.

Requirements (working draft)

  • Protects a road or triathlon bike through checked-baggage handling.
  • Total mass of about half the lightest commercial hard case.
  • Within standard airline oversize-baggage dimensions.
  • Can be built with student-accessible composites methods.

Plan

  1. Benchmark the weight, dimensions, and construction of existing hard and soft cases.
  2. Choose materials: skin layups and core options, sized with sandwich-beam stiffness calculations.
  3. CAD the contoured shell around a reference bike envelope, with a parting line and hardware.
  4. Test coupons: lay up flat sandwich panels and check stiffness and impact behavior.
  5. Prototype the shell on a foam plug, then weigh and drop-test it.
