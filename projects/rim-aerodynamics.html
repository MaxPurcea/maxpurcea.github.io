Princeton Carbon Works · Summer 2026

Parametric Rim Aerodynamics

Parametric CAD and CFD used to reduce triathlon wheel drag across the crosswind conditions riders actually see in a race.

Objective

Improve the aerodynamics of the company's 75 mm and 95 mm triathlon rims. Most rim profiles are tuned at 0° yaw, but crosswinds put a racing wheel at a range of angles, so the goal was to find the rim shape with the lowest drag across a race rather than at a single angle.

Approach

I built a parametric rim profile in SolidWorks and Fusion 360, controlled by maximum width, the position of maximum width, and the shoulder and trailing-edge spline lengths. Each variation was simulated in NablaFlow AeroCloud from 0° to 20° yaw, and a Python script weighted the results by how much time a triathlete spends at each angle to rank the designs.

Findings

Wider rims performed better. Drag was lowest at a maximum width of about 115% of tire width, compared with the roughly 105% common in the industry.

Optimizing at 0° alone was not enough. Profiles that tested best at 0° were often not the best once yaw weighting was applied, and on the deeper 95 mm rim the shoulder length and the position of maximum width became more influential at higher angles.

The results and a ranked set of candidate profiles were delivered to the engineering team for the next round of development.
