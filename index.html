Princeton Carbon Works · Summer 2026

Parametric Rim Aerodynamics

Parametric study of 75 and 95 mm triathlon rim profiles defined by four geometric parameters. Each candidate was evaluated with full yaw-range CFD, and drag was weighted by time spent at each yaw angle in racing. Maximum rim width was identified as the dominant parameter.

Cornell Lightweight Crew

For five and a half years, I've spent a few hours most Mondays through Saturdays placing a blade in the water and moving in sync with seven others. That time away from screens, school, friends, and even family gives me space to focus, reflect, breathe, and push my body and mind. To me, rowing is doing something uncomfortable so often that it becomes normal, and that has taught me to accept discomfort whenever it comes.

I've been lucky to experience the full range of what this sport has to offer, from cold early mornings to spectacular sunsets over Cayuga Lake. I found a part of my day worth sacrificing everything else for, because it holds a balance of effort and satisfaction. I believe you cannot truly appreciate the highs of life without feeling the lows.

About Me

I am pursuing a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering at Cornell University, with an expected graduation in May 2028. My primary interests are aerodynamics, lightweight structures, and design for manufacturing. During Summer 2026 I was an engineering intern at Princeton Carbon Works, where I performed parametric CFD studies of carbon triathlon wheel rims and prepared GD&T manufacturing drawings for a carbon frame.

At Cornell I am a member of the mold sub-team of the Cornell Concrete Canoe project team, which designs and builds a competition canoe each year. For the 2025–26 season the team produced its foam mold in-house using a hot-wire cutting process. I am also a varsity athlete on Cornell Lightweight Crew, training more than 20 hours per week alongside a full engineering course load.
